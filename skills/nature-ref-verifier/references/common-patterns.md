# Common Reference Verification Patterns

<!-- English edition: modified 2026-10-20; see EDITION-NOTICE.md at package root. -->

## Publication years

A DOI's digits are not a reliable publication-year field. Distinguish the online
publication date, assigned issue or volume year, and the date of a conference or
proceedings edition. Use the official metadata for the exact version being cited
and the requested citation style. For example, a paper posted online in late 2024
may appear in a 2025 volume. Explain this transition rather than treating the two
dates as evidence of a false citation.

## Author identity and order

Resolve a DOI or find the publisher's paper page and compare the full author list
in its original order. A completely different first author can identify a wrong
DOI or fabricated citation. Distinguish a complete list with omitted authors from
an intentionally shortened list using `et al.` under the chosen style.

Preserve diacritics, compound surnames, particles, and author-provided name forms.
Do not assume that reordering parts of a Spanish or Portuguese compound surname
is equivalent. Where non-Latin names are explicitly in scope, check the original
record for visually similar characters rather than guessing from transliteration.
Regional databases can supplement publisher or institutional records when needed.

## Pages and article identifiers

Compare the cited publication version. Preprints, early-access records,
proceedings, and final journal articles can have different pagination. Check
whether the field is a page range or an article number. Letters in identifiers
such as `101880T` must not be replaced with similar-looking digits. Watch for a
range copied accidentally from the neighbouring reference.

## DOI identity and coverage

A syntactically valid DOI can identify an entirely different article after a
single-digit transcription error. Compare the resolved title and authors, not
just the resolver's HTTP status. A missing Crossref result is not a demonstrated
invalid DOI: try the DOI resolver, the relevant registry, and the publisher.
Legacy IEEE records and region-specific publications may require direct source
checks. If multiple versions have distinct DOIs, cite the DOI for the version
actually used; do not collapse a preprint and a final article without explanation.

## Proceedings and theses

Verify the official conference/proceedings title, edition, publication year,
event year, and pagination as separate fields. Apply the target reference style
instead of universally substituting event year for publication year. For theses,
prefer the degree-granting institution or its repository; older works may not be
digitized or have a DOI. Missing digital access means unverifiable, not erroneous.

## Manuals, technical reports, and historical names

Confirm the organization, document title, report or product/version number, and
release year for the edition being cited. Use the document-type marker required
by the selected reference style rather than imposing one scheme. Preserve the
publisher or institution name used when the work was issued; a successor name
must not be retroactively substituted without a style requirement.

## Reporting uncertainty

Report original value, source value, evidence link, publication version, and the
reason for any unresolved conflict. Never force agreement by merging distinct
works or by replacing an inaccessible record with a plausible-looking one.
