# Calibrating Proposal Commitments

Use this pattern when exploratory possibilities have been written as guaranteed
deliverables. Lowering commitment means matching claims to evidence, not merely
deleting useful research questions. It is especially relevant inside an approved
project or when theoretical, chemical, engineering, or ethical constraints make a
strong promise untenable.

## Three levels

| Level | Evidence and scope | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Core deliverable | Literature support and available conditions justify a concrete planned output | Establish, determine, characterize; distinguish planned from completed work |
| Cautious exploration | Some support exists, but transfer to the target system is risky | Explore feasibility; test whether the relationship holds |
| Boundary confirmation | The task is to decide whether a route is feasible | Determine an operating window and Go/No-Go criteria |

## Procedure

List every major commitment and assign its level. Keep supported deliverables as
the core. Downgrade uncertain innovations to exploration, add failure criteria
and a fallback, and rewrite the document's positioning sentence to reflect the
result. Make conditions explicit: "if X fails the criterion, retain baseline Y".

For a high-risk intervention, replace a promise that method X will control the
target and deliver a stable process with a staged plan: use X for state detection
and feasibility checks first; attempt active control or optimization only after
the Go/No-Go criterion passes. This is justified when the literature demonstrates
measurement only, intervention changes boundary conditions, and existing evidence
does not separate the intended effect from the perturbation. Preserve a
well-supported baseline as the main route.

## Review and handoff

When content review reveals a P0 commitment problem, a useful sequence is draft,
two or three relevant specialist reviews, a prioritized revision brief,
user-confirmed scope, targeted editing instructions, author revision, and final
verification. Do not require this entire chain for ordinary proposals without a
demonstrated commitment problem. An external-editor handoff is a reviewable
artifact and does not itself authorize sending messages or files to another
service.
