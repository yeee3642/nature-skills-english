# Writing Within an Approved Proposal

<!-- English edition: modified 2026-10-20; see EDITION-NOTICE.md at package root. -->

Use this mode when a funded or otherwise approved project fixes the scientific
purpose, work packages, terminology, and scope, while the current task is to
deepen a research or execution plan inside those constraints.

## Intake and scope

Recognize requests such as "the grant is approved; I need a plan based on it" or
"do not change its aims". The user may share excerpts rather than a confidential
full proposal. Collect the supplied abstract, relevant literature context,
research tasks, objectives, questions, methods, route, contribution statements,
and expected outcomes before planning; respect an instruction to keep receiving
information. Mark the source location of each piece, identify the current author's
work package, and extract hard constraints such as performance targets, material
or operating ranges, prohibited claims, and formatting requirements. Ask only for
the missing information that actually blocks the requested scope.

## How this differs from ordinary composition

The research question and work-package structure are already set. Deepen them
without introducing a competing purpose or claiming independent novelty. Use the
proposal's existing references plus supplied evidence; a project commitment is
not itself a published finding. Changes outside the approved scope require the
user's direction.

Preserve task numbering and defined terminology. Existing bibliography numbers
may be retained when explicitly mapped to the proposal's reference list. Describe
the current work as a concrete part of the team question, not as an effort to
supersede it.

## Add execution detail where justified

Useful additions include an experimental matrix, gradients and replicates,
temperature or time points, method parameters, combined characterization,
success/failure criteria, fallback routes, and an independent route to the needed
samples or data. Match the detail to the plan's stage; do not invent laboratory
conditions merely to make the plan look complete.

If the author has a method absent from the approved proposal, embed it as a
candidate means of fulfilling an existing task, evaluation, or monitoring need.
Use wording such as "assess transferability to the target system" rather than
claiming a first invention without support.

## Independent execution

For parallel work packages, state how inputs can be obtained without depending
on another team's unfinished results, where model parameters and boundary
conditions come from, and which available platform supports the evaluation.
Describe later cross-validation with other packages without silently making
their future results a prerequisite.

## Foundation files

In `01_research_canon.md`, separate literature facts from approved-project
constraints. In `02_evidence_table.md`, identify claims originating from the
proposal, supplied reviews, and inference. Build `03_argument_map.md` from the
approved question and gap. Preserve numbering in `04_section_contracts.md` and
record the novelty and scope limits in `05_style_guide.md`.

## Failure modes

Do not cite a project application as if it were a published paper. Do not design
other work-package leaders' experiments or promise their outputs. A route marked
untested remains a hypothesis. Include a fallback for exploratory methods.
Project-wide publication, patent, or training targets are not automatically
appropriate personal research deliverables; prefer the datasets, methods,
processes, or criteria the current work can support. If evidence rules out a
route, remove it from core objectives or explicitly downgrade it; extra prose
must not disguise an infeasible route as a committed deliverable.
