## Claim ceiling before wording

The **claim ceiling** is the strongest conclusion current evidence and design support, together with conclusions they cannot support. It applies beyond correlation versus causation, including qualitative interpretation and historical reconstruction.

Every route needs a can-support and cannot-support sentence. Match each **verb** to the available **evidence**:

| Evidence | Defensible task | Unsupported leap |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews | Interpret participants' meanings and narrated experiences | Estimate population proportions from interviews alone |
| One-time survey | Describe observed associations | Observe within-person change or establish causal effects without a defensible design |
| Institutional documents | Analyse formal categories and representations | Treat formal statements as implementation experience |
| Platform logs | Describe recorded visible actions | Infer motives or meanings automatically |
| Surviving archives | Reconstruct documented processes within preservation bounds | Represent actors never recorded, retained, or accessible |

Do not retain broad theoretical language after quietly narrowing the empirical construct to match convenient materials.

## Concept distinctions and five argument errors

Use the twelve distinctions in `01-foundations.md` before drafting. Interest is not importance; topic is not problem; problem is not question; few studies is not a gap; unused methods are not contribution; available records are not valid observations.

Check five recurring failures:

1. **Exact-combination fallacy:** claiming novelty because one country, population, variable, and method have not appeared together.
2. **Construct drift:** asking about institutional trust while observing satisfaction or service use.
3. **Decorative theory:** listing theoretical names without competing expectations.
4. **Evidence overpromise:** promising population effects, causality, or complete processes from materials that support only description or meaning.
5. **Circular contribution:** importance rests on a gap whose importance rests only on not yet being studied.

## Status words must not be promoted

Keep `UNKNOWN ≠ PASS`, `DOCUMENTED ≠ ACCESS-CONFIRMED`, `SURVIVE ≠ novel`, `PARK ≠ bad idea`, `PASS ≠ permanent approval`, and `REJECT ≠ wasted work` visible.

Do not create a composite status. Step 6 SURVIVE, route DOCUMENTED, access dependency UNKNOWN, Step 7 PARK, and current gate REVISE can all apply without contradiction.

## Honest access wording

| State | Honest wording | Next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| NAMED | The source is identified; content and access remain unchecked. | Official catalogue or source documentation |
| DOCUMENTED | Documentation confirms relevant content; personal access remains unconfirmed. | Eligibility, permission, costs, location, timing |
| ACCESS-CONFIRMED | Lawful access is confirmed for this researcher's conditions. | An authorised small retrieval or material check |
| TESTED | A compliant minimal reality check has occurred. | Revise design and workload from actual observations |

Avoid an unsupported expectation that data will be obtainable. Give access, archive, recruitment, and restricted-data dependencies a decision date and fallback. With no fallback, state the main project risk directly.

Provisional application writing must still specify the knowledge problem, why it matters, supported/unsupported claims, access/ethics/skills dependencies, and how failure would change the question or design. A contribution hypothesis is not established novelty.

## Red flags

### Step 1: Search Space

Unsupported importance or under-research claims; a page of paraphrased interest without concept separation; dozens of unrelated concepts; premature titles/questions; automatic methods; assumed causal direction; ignored language, travel, or participant-access constraints; guessed facts.

### Step 2: Research Landscape

Author-by-author abstracts; exclusively supportive sources; consensus without construct/sample/context limits; prestige or citations substituting for evidence; methods inferred from titles; no-results converted into first-ever claims; apparently similar words hiding different constructs; quantitative-only coverage of a field with important archival or qualitative traditions; endless search expansion without clearer structure.

### Steps 3–5: Problems, evidence, and candidates

A gap justified only by few studies; mechanism used as a theoretical label; unobserved sequence promised by one-time data; variable names treated as measurement validity; public records assumed complete or ethically unrestricted; candidate differences limited to locations or methods; DOCUMENTED promoted to solved access; contribution reduced to filling a gap.

### Step 6: Academic challenge

Few results treated as originality; decisive novelty/rejection from metadata or abstracts; searches restricted to the exact location/population; seven identical generic attacks; causal standards imposed on every interpretive study; complex models excusing construct mismatch; redesign meaning only more variables, sample, or methods; a fatal failure averaged away; critic choosing a winner.

### Steps 7–8: Feasibility and comparison

Unknown ethics or access rewritten as permission; fixed three-paper or mixed-method PhD requirements; testing desired findings instead of reality conditions; hard failures offset by enthusiasm; parked directions described as startable; unverified named-supervisor fit; scores and rankings; fatal objections removed from Topic Cards; success forecasts replacing trade-off discussion.

## Literature and argument structure

Organise literature by question families, explanations, debate, and boundaries. A paper appears when it supports a structural judgement; the bibliography is not the argument's skeleton.

Seek and write contradictions, nulls, conditional results, reversals, and adjacent terminology. Establish whether apparent conflicts reflect genuinely competing findings or different populations, constructs, windows, and scales. Retain each consequential source's reading level.

For an argument, separately examine novelty, constructs, theory, evidence, method logic, alternatives, and contribution. State one strongest objection that could change the candidate's fate, why it matters, and what would answer it. Retain disagreement; no majority vote can cure fatal evidence mismatch.

Distinguish repairable narrowing, construct correction, lower claims, changed comparisons, or respecified mechanisms from hard failures: the problem is already resolved, core evidence is incompatible, contribution depends solely on an exact combination, or every repair creates another project.

## Revision patterns and human decisions

| Situation | Write instead of overclaiming |
|---|---|
| No close work found | No close work was located within these named sources, terms, dates, and languages; this does not establish absence. |
| Abstract-only reading | The abstract suggests this interpretation; methods remain unchecked (ABSTRACT LEVEL). |
| Unconfirmed personal access | Relevant content is documented; personal access requires the stated verification by the decision date. |
| Meanings-focused interviews | Participants describe or interpret this process; the material does not estimate a population effect. |
| Missing decisive conditions | PARK, with the missing condition and explicit reopening trigger. |
| Rejected direction | REJECT, with grounds and closest work retained to prevent repetition. |
| Bounded academic survival | SURVIVE within this audit; novelty remains unproven. |
| New method | Explain the specific knowledge difficulty its observations or inference resolve. |

AI can prepare a request but cannot authorise ethics, access, or supervision. Describe required supervision expertise and resources before naming a person. Current official information or confirmed contact is needed to claim recruitment, willingness, or fit.

Personal experience can explain motivation; the landscape must establish scholarly importance. Preserve the researcher's values, acceptable risk, and long-term commitments as their decisions.
