## Scope and limits

This workbench serves prospective PhD applicants, master's students exploring doctoral directions, and early doctoral researchers whose topics remain broad or unstable. It covers social science and adjacent humanities without assuming programming knowledge or a preferred method.

It provides a route from a bounded search space through literature, unresolved problems, evidence, candidates, academic challenge, and feasibility to a shortlist. It does not supply a guaranteed correct topic or replace reading, methodological training, supervision, ethics review, access permission, or participant relationships.

A convincing sentence, a confident assistant, and a completed worksheet are not evidence that a project can proceed.

## Interest to contribution

Connect the phenomenon that matters to the researcher, a knowledge difficulty, an answerable question, appropriate evidence, a defensible design, and a worthwhile contribution.

| Term | Meaning | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | A phenomenon or experience that sustains attention | What could I spend years understanding? |
| Topic | A bounded scholarly territory | Which phenomena, actors, materials, and settings are included? |
| Research problem | A consequential uncertainty or explanatory failure | What remains unclear, and why does resolving it matter? |
| Research question | A precise inquiry evidence can advance | What will I explain, compare, understand, or trace? |
| Evidence/design | Observations, materials, and inference that answer the question | What traces distinguish competing explanations? |
| Contribution | The understanding a credible answer changes | What would readers understand differently? |

An interest in generative AI and university education does not identify a problem, population, process, or evidential task. A survey item on AI-use frequency does not automatically measure dependency, learning, judgement, or teaching relationships.

If design supplies a weak proxy, wrong unit, or wrong time window, return to the question. If revising it removes its knowledge consequence, return to the problem.

## Twelve diagnostic distinctions

| Keep separate | Operational check |
|---|---|
| Interest and topic | Delimit phenomenon, process, and boundaries. |
| Topic and research problem | Identify a specific difficulty in existing understanding. |
| Research problem and research question | Convert the difficulty into an evidence-addressable task. |
| Research gap and few studies | Explain the knowledge consequence of missing work. |
| Novelty and an unstudied exact combination | Specify contradiction, mechanism, measurement, or a newly observable process. |
| Interesting and important | Explain whose understanding changes. |
| Important and answerable | Establish suitable, lawful, timely evidence. |
| Available source and valid evidence | Check constructs, meanings, units, timing, and coverage. |
| Correlation and mechanism | Identify actors, sequence, and distinct observable traces. |
| Advanced method and strong contribution | Explain the knowledge difficulty the method resolves. |
| Feasible study and PhD-worthy project | Show coherent development around a central puzzle. |
| AI judgement and researcher judgement | Preserve human commitments, ethics, risk, and final choice. |

Broad terms can conceal distinct constructs. Housing instability and social embeddedness, for example, each contain non-interchangeable meanings; connecting labels does not establish a coherent relationship. Returning to concepts is useful, rather than merely polishing the question.

Allow ideas to weaken. Preserve objections, failed routes, and redesign options rather than defending the starting point indefinitely.

## AI and researcher responsibilities

AI can search, organise, verify, compare, and challenge. It can expand terminology, find counterevidence, compare documentation, expose assumptions, and prepare inspectable records.

Researchers retain intellectual ownership, disciplinary positioning, ethics responsibility, acceptable risk, personal commitment, supervision judgement, and final choice. AI cannot confer novelty, access permission, ethics approval, participant trust, admission, or funding.

| AI can lead | Shared work | Researcher must own |
|---|---|---|
| Search and terminology expansion | Organisation and interpretation of disputed evidence | Construct refinement and intellectual commitment |
| Source comparison | Comparing alternatives and verification planning | Disciplinary positioning and ethics |
| Contradiction hunting and checks | Preparing supervisor questions | Acceptable burden, supervision judgement, final choice |

Consult a relevant person when disciplinary weight cannot be judged, decisive full texts or documentation are inaccessible, participants or materials are sensitive, training burden is uncertain, or the project depends on a supervisor, institution, or source holder.

## Evidence levels

Keep the depth of each source inspection visible:

| Level | What has actually been checked |
|---|---|
| METADATA ONLY | Bibliographic identity, such as title, author, and year |
| ABSTRACT LEVEL | Authors' summary; methods remain insufficiently inspected |
| FULL-TEXT LEVEL | Methods, materials, sample, results, and limitations in the paper |
| PRIMARY/OFFICIAL | Original questionnaires, archives, access rules, policy texts, or source documentation |

These describe source inspection. Evidence State describes verification of a proposed research route. Neither substitutes for the other.

## Entry modes and false starts

| Starting material | Mode | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Interest without a bounded phenomenon | Mode A | Complete Step 1, then Steps 2–8 |
| Broad topic or relationship | Mode B | Disentangle concepts and boundaries in Step 1, then Steps 2–8 |
| Tentative RQ with partial rationale | Mode C — AUDIT ROUTE | Targeted audit of Steps 1–4, then challenge and feasibility |

Mode A may begin with personal or practical motivation; this does not establish scholarly importance. Mode B must not treat an A-and-B phrase as a justified question. Mode C exposes problem, concepts, actors/materials, time, evidence, explanations, and claims; unknowns remain unknown. It does not protect the original wording.

A surviving Mode C question still needs at least one serious redesign and one strong alternative addressing the same difficulty.

### Two false starts

A dataset is an evidential resource. Inspect what it records and omits, whom it covers, units and timing, and how its production shapes observations.

A preferred method is useful only when it serves a knowledge problem. Ask what unresolved phenomenon the dataset or method could help understand. If unclear, enter Mode A.

## Gates and the topic funnel

Each step ends with `PASS`, `REVISE`, or `STOP`. `PASS` supports progression using current evidence and version. `REVISE` specifies what to change or verify. `STOP` preserves reasons and returns to an earlier step, another direction, or changed conditions. From Step 5 onward, `PARK` can preserve a direction lacking decisive evidence or conditions.

Outputs are a one-page Search Space, Research Landscape, 3–7 Problem Cards, 1–3 examined evidence routes, usually 6–12 Candidate RQ Cards, Novelty Audit and Reviewer 2 Sheets, PhD Feasibility Dossiers, and at most three Topic Cards.

Do not collapse decisions into scores. A candidate may survive academic criticism while remaining parked because access is unknown.

At each step, record unknowns, search contradictions and alternatives, preserve evidence depth and failed searches, and replace obsolete entries in Current Research State. Begin with entry mode, original input, hard constraints, and assumptions open to challenge.
