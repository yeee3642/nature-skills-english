## Step 6: Attack the Ideas

Use frozen Candidate RQ Cards, existing sources and reading levels, search boundaries/failures, completed redesigns, and the researcher's concerns. When practical, separate criticism from the generation conversation so the critic is not defending its own candidates.

Search closest work through alternative terminology, adjacent concepts, key authors, and citation chains. Compare the same knowledge uncertainty, competing explanations, evidence logic, and contribution claim. Different countries or populations do not automatically establish a difference.

Preserve date, databases/materials, time/language scope, queries, omissions, and failed searches. Not found within this boundary does not mean never studied.

### Seven separate academic attacks

| Dimension | Decisive question |
|---|---|
| Novelty | What has the closest work already answered, and what consequential difference remains? |
| Constructs | Do target concepts, indicators, proxies, meanings, and materials match? |
| Theory | Do explanations predict distinct traces, rather than decorate the question? |
| Evidence | Do units, timing, coverage, and source production support the question? |
| Method logic | Can proposed analysis perform the needed comparison, interpretation, or process reconstruction? |
| Alternatives | What else produces the observations, and what negative case distinguishes it? |
| Contribution | What understanding changes beyond adding a case? |

Apply the inferential standards appropriate to quantitative, qualitative, archival, documentary, corpus, ethnographic, or mixed materials. Causal identification is not the universal standard.

Write one strongest rejection reason: if true, why does it defeat the candidate, and what evidence or redesign could answer it? Do not conceal it beneath generic lists about sample size, bias, and generalisability.

Repairable issues include narrowing the question, correcting constructs, lowering claims, changing comparison, or respecifying mechanism. Hard failures include equivalent prior work resolving the uncertainty, incompatible evidence and constructs, contribution based only on an exact combination, or repairs that necessarily create a different project.

Preserve disagreements. A novelty difference cannot outvote a fatal construct mismatch. No average scores or majority votes.

### Quick and Deep Runs

Quick Run screens the full pool for closest work, construct mismatch, evidence/claim mismatch, and empty contribution. Save the strongest objection, hidden assumption, decisive action, preliminary disposition, and gate. It does not replace Deep Run for survivors.

Only `SURVIVE` candidates or completed explicit redesigns enter Deep Run. Deep Run produces a Novelty Audit and Reviewer 2 Sheet for each, including seven attacks, counterevidence, hidden assumptions, claim ceiling, strongest rejection reason, redesign, decisive verification, and disposition.

A decisive rejection should rely on sufficiently inspected evidence, preferably full text for the closest work. Insufficient decisive evidence calls for `PARK` or `REDESIGN`, not confident rejection from abstracts.

## Step 6 dispositions and gates

`SURVIVE`: the core knowledge rationale survives the declared audit; novelty is not proven.

`REDESIGN`: the problem may remain but question, constructs, evidence, mechanism, or claim must change. Complete the specified revision and re-attack it.

`PARK`: decisive full text, source verification, or expert judgement is missing. Save the reopening condition.

`REJECT`: adequate evidence defeats the core academic rationale. Keep the rejection record.

Gate `PASS` requires at least one genuine survivor, transparent sources/search bounds, and no averaged-away hard failure. `REVISE` requires specified revision or decisive evidence. `STOP` applies when all candidates fail and repairs create different projects; return to Steps 3–5 according to the failing link.

Only survivors and completed redesigns proceed to Step 7. Keep parked/rejected records outside the active pool.

## Step 7: Can This Become a PhD?

Academic survival does not establish personal or doctoral feasibility. Use the surviving question, claim ceiling, evidence route/state, preliminary design, access/ethics/safety information, time/funding/language/location, skills, supervision/resources, long-term interests, and unacceptable burdens.

### Eight hard gates

| Hard gate | What must be established |
|---|---|
| Core evidence | At least one route observes the core problem rather than a convenient proxy. |
| Access and permission | Personal access is confirmed or has a realistic, verifiable path. |
| Ethics | Risks have a plausible compliant management route, not assumed future approval. |
| Researcher safety | Physical, digital, legal, political, and emotional risks are acceptable. |
| Time | Decisive dependencies can be resolved before relevant application/early-PhD deadlines. |
| Necessary skills | Missing skills can be learned or supported in time. |
| Supervision and resources | The required expertise, facilities, and institutional support can realistically coexist. |
| Scientific information value | Successful execution could still answer the central problem. |

For each use `CONFIRMED`, `RESOLVABLE DEPENDENCY`, `UNKNOWN`, or `FAIL`, with evidence/date, verifier, action, and decision date. Unknown does not pass; a failure cannot be cancelled by interest, contribution, or method advantages.

### Scope and dependencies

Specify a **minimum viable study**: the smallest credible study that advances the central problem, with explicit supported and unsupported claims. Then show a coherent thesis development route around the same puzzle and identify overexpansion.

PhD scale does not require exactly three studies, mixed methods, or one thesis format. A monograph, ethnography, article thesis, and computational project can have different structures.

Create a twelve-month dependency plan, not twelve filled task boxes. For each decisive dependency record prerequisites, responsible person, earliest verification, required evidence, decision date, and failure alternative.

Propose a **minimal reality check** within permissions and ethics: inspect a codebook and small sample, test archive catalogues and limited materials, confirm access with a gatekeeper, evaluate recruitment channels, or rehearse a process on non-sensitive material. It tests access, material existence, workload, and procedure; it does not seek significance or desired outcomes and does not bypass consent or restrictions.

Personal interest, life arrangements, identity risk, emotional burden, and career goals are design boundaries. AI does not decide what the researcher ought to tolerate.

Describe required supervision capabilities and institutional resources. Do not invent an available or willing named supervisor. Claims about recruitment, resources, and access require current official evidence or the researcher's confirmed contact.

### Run depth and save

Quick Run records the eight hard gates, evidence/claim check, decisive unknowns, minimal actions, minimum-study outline, fatal failure condition, and `ENTER DEEP RUN / REDESIGN / PARK / REJECT`. It does not build the annual plan or shortlist.

Deep Run produces the full Feasibility Dossier: hard-gate evidence, minimum study, lawful reality check, PhD scale, dependency plan, training, supervision/resources, personal limits, fatal failure condition, fallback, human confirmations, disposition, and gate.

## Step 7 dispositions and gates

`PROCEED TO COMPARISON`: no untreated hard failure, realistic paths and decision dates for critical dependencies, a startable minimum study, coherent development, and visible personal/supervision bounds.

`REDESIGN`: scope, evidence, access, training order, ethics, timing, or development must change.

`PARK`: conditions may change, but the project cannot yet realistically start or be judged.

`REJECT`: the project is infeasible within acceptable bounds or lacks scientific information value.

Gate `PASS` supports only candidates disposed `PROCEED TO COMPARISON`. `REVISE` requires a decisive check or redesign, sometimes returning to Steps 4–5. `STOP` applies to unmanaged core failures or a project that remains too small or overexpanded at any credible scale. Preserve the fatal failure condition; sunk effort is not a reason to continue.

## Step 8: Build the Final Shortlist

Check eligibility before comparison: Step 6 `SURVIVE` or completed redesign; Step 7 `PROCEED TO COMPARISON`; no untreated hard-gate `FAIL`; visible evidence states, claim ceiling, objection, and fatal failure condition.

Retain one to three substantively different directions. Do not lower standards or duplicate the same question to fill three places. Preserve non-finalists as `REDESIGN`, `PARK`, or `REJECT` with reasons.

Use written judgements and evidence to compare intellectual attraction, knowledge importance, answerability/claim ceiling, contribution logic, evidence/state, hard risks, ethics/safety, learning/execution burden, supervision/resources, minimum study, PhD development, and fatal failure condition.

No item scores, totals, weights, stars, algorithmic ranks, success probabilities, or automatic winner. A hard failure cannot be balanced against strengths.

Separate unknowns reducible by new evidence from value and life trade-offs the researcher must own. Give each finalist one decisive next check and explain how success or failure would change the shortlist.

Complete all eighteen Topic Card fields in `06-worksheets.md`. Retain decisive evidence levels, access, unknowns, and risks; link full search logs, variable identifiers, and technical inventories instead of copying them into the card.

Do not silently regenerate questions or draft downstream applications during this step. Do not predict admission, funding, publication, or PhD success.

## Final gates and examples

`PASS`: one to three eligible, genuinely different directions have complete cards, honest trade-offs, visible unknowns, and decisive next checks. Stop discovery; use the cards for supervisor discussion or verification.

`REVISE`: eligibility checks are incomplete, differences cosmetic, decisive unknowns hidden, or cards conceal risks. Return to the missing step and update only fields changed by new evidence.

`STOP`: no eligible direction remains. Do not force a proposal; return to the strongest earlier problem or wait for a stated condition to change.

### Hypothetical teaching examples — not verified topics

Planning documents that increasingly mention public participation may reveal changing formal categories without establishing residents' actual influence. Meeting records or a narrower question may be needed: `REDESIGN`.

A promising workplace help-seeking question may remain `PARK` when organisational entry and employment-related confidentiality cannot yet be confirmed. Replacing observed interactions with policy documents is not a valid access workaround.
