## Five separate status systems

### Workflow Gate

Question: Can the current step advance?

| Label | Meaning and action |
|---|---|
| PASS | Current output supports the next step; save it and advance. |
| REVISE | The direction may remain, but revise this or an earlier step and reassess. |
| STOP | Preserve reasons, stop this route, and return, change direction, or wait for stated conditions. |

From Step 5 onward, PARK can suspend a direction pending decisive evidence or conditions. A PASS applies to the current evidence and version, not permanent approval.

### Evidence State

Question: How far has this proposed research route actually been verified?

| Label | Meaning | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| NAMED | Only the source name or mention is known. | Content, access, or fit |
| DOCUMENTED | Reliable catalogue, questionnaire, codebook, archive guide, or official page confirms content. | This researcher's personal ability to obtain/use it |
| ACCESS-CONFIRMED | Personal lawful access, approvals, fees, location, and conditions confirmed. | Tested material quality, recruitment, or analysis |
| TESTED | An authorised small retrieval, inspection, recruitment test, or procedure check completed. | Full feasibility or desirable findings |

States require evidence and cannot be skipped.

### Academic Survival

Question: What happened to the candidate after Step 6 academic challenge?

| Label | Meaning and action |
|---|---|
| SURVIVE | The strongest objection has not defeated the core rationale; proceed to feasibility, without claiming proven novelty. |
| REDESIGN | The problem may remain, but question, constructs, evidence, mechanism, or claim must change; revise and re-attack. |
| PARK | Decisive full text, source verification, or expertise is missing; record reopening conditions. |
| REJECT | Adequate evidence defeats the core academic rationale; retain reasons rather than repackaging it. |

### PhD Feasibility Dependency

Question: What is the current condition of one hard dependency?

| Label | Meaning and action |
|---|---|
| CONFIRMED | Current evidence shows the condition is satisfied; retain basis and date. |
| RESOLVABLE DEPENDENCY | Not satisfied, but a realistic route exists; name action, owner, evidence, and decision date. |
| UNKNOWN | Insufficient basis; verify rather than assume passage. |
| FAIL | The condition fails without an acceptable route in the current design; stop, redesign, or park. |

One FAIL cannot be offset by intellectual interest, contribution, or methodological strengths.

### PhD Feasibility Disposition

Question: Can the completed Step 7 candidate enter final comparison?

| Label | Meaning and action |
|---|---|
| PROCEED TO COMPARISON | No untreated failure, realistic critical-dependency paths, and coherent PhD development; enter Step 8. |
| REDESIGN | Scope, evidence, dependencies, training, ethics, or development must change; repeat feasibility after revision. |
| PARK | Conditions might change, but the project cannot presently start or be judged. |
| REJECT | Infeasible within acceptable boundaries or insufficient scientific information value; preserve reasons. |

Step 7 Quick Run also uses `ENTER DEEP RUN` to route a candidate to its full dossier. That local routing label is not a replacement for the final feasibility disposition.

## Six distinctions to preserve

- `UNKNOWN ≠ PASS`: missing knowledge needs a verification action, not an optimistic interpretation.
- `DOCUMENTED ≠ ACCESS-CONFIRMED`: official existence is not personal eligibility, permission, location, cost, or ethics confirmation.
- `SURVIVE ≠ novel`: a bounded challenge cannot prove worldwide absence of close work.
- `PARK ≠ bad idea`: decisive evidence or conditions are missing; the idea need not be academically wrong.
- `PASS ≠ permanent approval`: new literature, changed access, ethics feedback, or testing can reopen the gate.
- `REJECT ≠ wasted work`: rejection preserves closest work, failure conditions, and boundaries, preventing repeat mistakes.

Do not create a composite status. A candidate can simultaneously have Step 6 SURVIVE, evidence DOCUMENTED, access dependency UNKNOWN, Step 7 PARK, and current gate REVISE. These answer different questions.

## Terminology

| Term | Working meaning |
|---|---|
| Interest | Sustained attraction; motivation rather than proof of importance. |
| Topic | A bounded territory that can contain multiple knowledge problems. |
| Research problem | Conflict, explanatory failure, invisibility, or measurement difficulty in existing knowledge; not merely few studies. |
| Research question | Precise task evidence can advance by explaining, comparing, tracing, or understanding. |
| Mechanism | How a phenomenon is produced, maintained, or changed: actors/conditions, sequence, observable traces. |
| Evidence level | Source-inspection depth: METADATA ONLY, ABSTRACT LEVEL, FULL-TEXT LEVEL, PRIMARY/OFFICIAL. |
| Evidence state | Route-verification progress: NAMED, DOCUMENTED, ACCESS-CONFIRMED, TESTED. |
| Claim ceiling | Maximum supported conclusion and explicit unsupported conclusions. |
| Source-generation bias | How recording, recruitment, preservation, publication, deletion, and platforms determine presence and absence. |
| Contribution | Change in knowledge, explanation, measurement, or theory; not a new place, sample, or method alone. |
| Minimum viable study | Smallest credible first study that advances the core problem. |
| Minimal reality check | Permission-compliant small check of access, materials, recruitment, workload, or procedure; not a search for significance. |
| PhD-sized | A startable first study and coherent development of one puzzle, avoiding a tiny isolated analysis or team-scale overreach. |
| Fatal failure condition | Decisive event requiring stopping or fundamental redesign. |
