## Step 4: Map Observable Evidence

Start with what must be observed to advance an unresolved problem, rather than famous datasets. Sources can exist yet be unable to answer the question.

### Required input

Provide the problem; what is known; competing meanings/explanations/mechanisms; necessary phenomena, experiences, events, relationships, or materials; units; time sequence/history; population/context bounds; known sources; access, language, budget, time, ethics, safety, skills constraints; and unacceptable evidential compromises.

A source known only by name remains `NAMED`.

### Check Ask

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Construct or meaning | Direct observation, defensible indicator, weak proxy, or mismatch? |
| Unit or material | Do persons, relationships, organisations, events, texts, or archive series match? |
| Timing | Can sequence, change, duration, or the relevant event be observed? |
| Coverage | Who or what is included, absent, or selectively retained? |
| Access | What route state is evidenced for this researcher? |
| Ethics | Can consent, privacy, vulnerability, platform rules, and researcher safety be managed? |
| Source-generation bias | How do recording, recruitment, preservation, publication, deletion, and platform processes shape observations? |
| Claim ceiling | What can the design support, and what explicitly remains unsupported? |

Consider surveys, panels, interviews, oral histories, focus groups, ethnography, observation, archives, policy/legal/organisational documents, media corpora, digital traces, administrative records, experiments, and authorised organisational records as appropriate. Do not rank evidence types by default or prescribe mixed methods for apparent completeness.

A variable label is not construct validation. Official participation language does not establish actual participation. One wave cannot observe within-person change. Retrospective accounts do not automatically recover event sequence. Administrative, archival, and platform records were produced for purposes other than the researcher's concepts.

## Evidence states and claim ceiling

| Evidence State | Required basis |
|---|---|
| NAMED | Only a name or mention; content and access unchecked. |
| DOCUMENTED | Reliable catalogue, questionnaire, codebook, archive guide, or official page confirms relevant content. |
| ACCESS-CONFIRMED | This researcher's lawful access, approvals, fees, location, and permissions confirmed. |
| TESTED | Authorised small retrieval, inspection, recruitment test, or workflow check actually completed. |

Do not skip states or confuse them with source reading levels. A statement that researchers may apply does not establish personal access. `DOCUMENTED ≠ ACCESS-CONFIRMED`; `TESTED` does not guarantee the full study or desired results.

For each route state what it can and cannot support. Interviews may explain meanings without population proportions. Cross-sectional surveys may describe association without individual change. Institutional documents show formal categories without directly showing implementation. Logs show visible behaviour without establishing motives.

Quick Run screens 2–4 routes and stops with a gate and minimal verification action. Deep Run examines 1–3 survivors using current primary/official documentation, recording dates, fit, access, ethics, bias, unknowns, complementary roles, stopping conditions, and next checks.

Complementary materials may address different layers. Do not automatically call agreement between rules, narratives, and records verification of the same fact.

## Step 4 gates and example

Quick `PASS` means at least one preliminary route merits deeper checking. Quick `REVISE` clarifies what must be observed. Quick `STOP` returns to Step 3 when routes measure the wrong thing, cannot be accessed, or are unacceptable.

Deep `PASS`: at least one route fits construct/meaning, unit, timing, and coverage; access and ethics have realistic paths; decisive unknowns have executable checks. This does not imply a completed pilot.

`REVISE`: weak measurement, wrong timing, unconfirmed access, insufficient ethics design, or evidence answering only a narrower question. Verify, replace evidence, lower claims, or return to Step 3. Do not replace the construct silently with a convenient proxy.

`STOP`: the core phenomenon is unobservable, access requires unrealistic agreements, recruitment is unsafe, materials cannot be used ethically, or every route answers a different question.

### Hypothetical teaching example — not a verified topic

For workers' experiences of account deactivation, interviews might examine notices, interpretations, appeals, and help-seeking. Public rules examine formal categories and promises. Appeal records, if lawfully obtainable, might show procedural stages. Public help posts capture visible narratives shaped by recommendation, deletion, and disclosure risks; public availability is not automatic consent to research.

Unconfirmed recruitment, privacy protection, and appeal-record access require `REVISE`, not a feasible-project claim. Preserve each route's actual state. The original handbook's housing-data illustration likewise remained at documented possibilities rather than tested access; it is not current dataset verification.

## Step 5: Generate Candidate Research Questions

Generate only after at least one consequential problem, one matching evidence route, and an explicit claim ceiling exist. If any is absent, stop and return `REVISE`.

Use the current Search Space, 1–3 prioritised problems with four-question summaries, surviving evidence routes/states, supported and unsupported claims, competing explanations, constraints, unknowns, and any Mode C question. Do not pass all prior chats.

### Three generation routes

| Route | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Problem-driven | Reduce an identified knowledge uncertainty. |
| Evidence-driven | Use new observations, sequence, comparison, materials, or meanings to make a substantive inquiry answerable. |
| Mechanism-driven | Distinguish processes or competing explanations through observable traces. |

Usually create 6–12 Candidate RQ Cards. Try all three routes seriously, with at least one meaningful candidate per applicable route; explain inapplicability rather than padding. Preserve failed routes and reasons.

Retain at least three candidates differing substantively in knowledge task, explanation, timing, scale, material, or contribution logic. Merge variants that only change location, population, dataset, method, or synonyms.

Evidence-driven does not mean studying whatever fields exist. Explain which previously missing observation changes the inquiry. A mechanism needs a process and competitor with distinguishable traces. For Mode C, retain the original without privilege, one serious redesign, and one strong alternative.

### Candidate RQ Card

Record identifier and route; knowledge problem; exact question; concepts/meanings or focus; actors/context/materials; unit; time/process bounds; evidence route/state; mechanism and competing explanation; observable distinctions; maximum claim; contribution hypothesis; likeliest fatal flaw; one pre-Step-6 check; differences from other cards; assumptions and unknowns.

Verbs must match evidence: do not promise population effects from meanings-focused interviews, absent actors' experiences from surviving formal archives, or within-person change from one observation.

## Candidate checks and gates

This stage generates and clarifies; it does not establish novelty, feasibility, PhD scale, supervisor fit, priorities, or a winner. Preserve evidence levels/states without upgrades.

`PASS`: explicit questions connect consequential problems with observable evidence, meaningful variation, serious generation routes, unknowns, and fatal risks.

`REVISE`: candidates are topics, cosmetic variants, method packaging, excessive claims, or mechanisms without traces. Return to Step 3 or 4 to repair the link.

`STOP`: none connects a consequential problem with observable evidence, or all rely on rejected Step 4 routes.

Freeze candidate cards for criticism rather than polishing apparent strength. Carry cards, generation failures, states, claim ceilings, and gate into Step 6; retain merged variants and old chats separately.
